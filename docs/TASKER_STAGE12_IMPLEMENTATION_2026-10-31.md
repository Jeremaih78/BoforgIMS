# Stage 12: continuous improvement, SOPs and recurring workflows

Implemented locally on 30–31 October 2026, Africa/Harare. This covers T12-01–T12-06. Deployment, sender authentication and the employee pilot are separate acceptance steps. Stage 13 has not started. No productivity uplift is established by task counts or these synthetic tests.

## Employee and manager workflow

Open **Improve** in Tasker navigation. The five tabs are Weekly review, SOP library, Recurring work, Idea review and Experiments. Now also links repeated carry-over to a decision and shows a closed-day cue.

1. **Weekly review:** choose My review or a currently visible team and a Monday. The agenda rechecks current blockers, unresolved help/handoff requests, pending goal-output acceptance and enabled stocktake approvals. Resolved, reassigned or inaccessible sources disappear immediately. Completed tasks and accepted goal outputs are separate measures. Personal reflections stay private; a manager's team note is explicitly shared with current authorized team readers. Historical references contain IDs, not cached private work text. Large lists disclose bounded coverage.
2. **Break the carry-over loop:** work planned/carried over on at least two earlier days since its last explicit decision is offered for review. Clarify its acceptance condition, split a small child task, delegate through existing assignment permissions, reschedule with a reason, or stop it. The reason enters shared task history. Employees without a qualified delegation target can request the existing help/handoff workflow. Decisions check the current task version and retain conflicting drafts. Nothing automatically rolls every unfinished task into tomorrow.
3. **SOP library:** create a personal SOP, or let an authorized manager publish one for a team. Include instructions, a definition of done and one checklist step per line. **Create my task** copies the selected current version into one ordinary Tasker task with a checklist and source-version link. Retrying that request opens the same task. A later publication appends a version; it does not edit existing task instructions, checklists or execution records. Archive/restore preserves evidence. The original sharing scope is fixed.
4. **Recurring work:** start from an SOP, name a qualified owner and choose daily, weekly or monthly intervals, local release time, timezone, optional end date and missed-work policy. New routines start paused. Resume deliberately. Rule changes require an explicit future-calendar rebase; pending cursor changes and rule history are audited. Pause preserves the pending cursor; resume applies the chosen missed-work policy. A dated exception records a shared reason and is retained with the calendar; it cannot rewrite an executed occurrence. To replace a calendar with different scope or historical exceptions, pause it and create a new routine.
5. **Idea review:** compare ideas you own or ideas already explicitly shared with the selected team. Related-text suggestions and shared tags are deterministic, bounded and optional; they never merge, delete or share captures. A capture owner can define a small experiment with owner, expected benefit, unit, baseline, target, improvement direction and review date. A team experiment requires the source idea to have been shared deliberately with that team. The original idea body and external URL are not copied into experiment work. Existing Stage 9 capture assistance remains available under its own permissions and activation switches; Stage 12 requires no AI call.
6. **Experiments:** finish the linked task against its acceptance condition, record a measured value and verifiable evidence, then have an authorized team manager accept the observation. A personal experiment is reviewed by its owner. Acceptance appends evidence; it does not overwrite the original observation or automatically award company-goal credit. Reopening, editing or moving the task makes the earlier proof historical. A team manager can align experiment ownership with a delegation already made in the same team/company; fresh evidence is required. Scope changes do not silently move the experiment's audience. For different team/private scope, preserve the old record and define new work deliberately.
7. **End day:** save today's private review, then select **Close my day**. A running Focus needs to be stopped explicitly, or the employee can authorize stopping it while closing. Closing creates the Stage 8 factual team submissions and report jobs. Duplicate submissions do not create a second occurrence of the same declaration. Closed days block new Focus starts until reopened. **Reopen my day** requires a private reason and appends a factual declaration revision. A correction to a closed review requires its current version and a private correction reason. Reflection-only edits preserve shared-report identity; changed shared facts produce append-only report revisions. Old dates remain read-only. Missing submissions remain explicit.

## Recurrence semantics and operating limits

- Identity is `(routine, local calendar date)`, protected by PostgreSQL uniqueness and row locks. Task creation, checklist/execution receipts, cursor advancement and the late job lease fence are atomic. A reclaimed worker cannot commit duplicate tasks.
- A release creates a pending task, never a timer, accepted time reservation, business transaction or accepted result. Due date is the occurrence's local date; release time is not presented as a fabricated deadline.
- Weekly intervals anchor to Monday and selected weekdays. Monthly calendars preserve the original day and clamp to month end. The timezone is explicit. Ambiguous and non-existent DST release times produce a skipped calendar receipt rather than a guessed instant.
- **Latest only:** skips older due dates and creates at most the latest due occurrence. **Skip past days:** creates only an eligible current-day occurrence. **Bounded catch-up:** processes at most 20 calendar occurrences per job. Long latest/skip ranges have a compact skipped-range receipt.
- **Skip while earlier work is open** is the default overlap policy. Choose overlapping tasks explicitly when appropriate. Skipping is an auditable outcome, not an endlessly rescheduled task.
- Future occurrences use the SOP version published by their scheduled instant; already-created tasks retain their version. Lost owner membership, permissions, SOP access or execution authority pauses generation for review.
- Existing worker/scheduler processes handle `routine.generate` with reference-only payloads. A fair scan cursor prevents later routines being starved. Future release times do not enqueue repair floods. Exhausted/failed/cancelled job budgets remain exhausted; repair of a successful no-effect job retains occurrence identity.

## Email first: implemented, activation off

The existing Stage 8 snapshots, recipient grants and NotificationDelivery ledger remain authoritative. `reports.smtp` adds a real Django SMTP adapter alongside the test adapter. Every send rechecks the recipient, current report/source proof, reporting policy, cutoff, quiet hours and work/focus suppression. New code does not activate live mail.

Three conditions must agree: the reporting/improvement pipeline is enabled, an authorized company manager chooses SMTP and explicitly enables that company's email policy, and the deployment owner enables `TASKER_REPORT_EMAIL_LIVE_ENABLED` after private sender setup. Missing inputs hold delivery. Company recipients still require an active OrganizationAccess grant with `can_receive_reports`; being a team manager alone does not select a top-manager email recipient.

Company test mode remains local even when SMTP is eligible for other companies. The SMTP adapter uses a stable Message-ID and a 1–15 second socket timeout. It commits an **UNKNOWN** intent before the network boundary. A timeout, crash, lost worker lease or failure to record provider acceptance cannot silently trigger another email. Unknown attempts require operator review; stable Message-ID is not claimed to provide provider-side deduplication. **PROVIDER_ACCEPTED** means SMTP accepted the message, not that it was delivered or read. Test acceptance means no real email was sent. WhatsApp remains deferred as selected by the owner.

Configure the reviewed sender privately through the deployment environment: `EMAIL_HOST`, `EMAIL_PORT`, `EMAIL_HOST_USER`, `EMAIL_HOST_PASSWORD`, `EMAIL_USE_TLS`, `EMAIL_USE_SSL`, `EMAIL_TIMEOUT` and `DEFAULT_FROM_EMAIL`. TLS and SSL must not both be enabled. Do not paste credential values into chat or commit them. Existing Django mail defaults are preserved for other IMS features; the default localhost sender cannot activate Tasker report delivery. The exact provider, approved sender, real recipient roster, cutoff/report days, retention and operating owner still need company setup. Use the existing report-policy screen for company choices.

```text
python manage.py check_tasker_report_readiness
```

This command is offline: it shows configuration booleans without addresses or credentials, sends no email, queries no database and leaves activation unchanged. It does not authenticate the provider or verify company policy/recipient grants. OpenAI replacement-key setup and the confirmed USD 0.50 employee/USD 5 company weekly AI limits are unchanged.

## Deployment and rollback

Migrations **0029_continuous_improvement** and **0030_improvement_email_policy** are additive. Rehearse the complete pending migration graph on a private production backup first; preceding Stage 9–11 migrations may also be pending. Apply normal permission setup with `setup_tasker_roles`, collect static assets with strict manifest storage and check public static-file permissions. The local synthetic preview was migrated; production was not.

Defaults are off: `TASKER_IMPROVEMENT_ENABLED`, `TASKER_RECURRENCE_ENABLED`, `TASKER_REPORT_EMAIL_LIVE_ENABLED`. Enable improvement for a reviewed deployment, then recurrence only after calendars, assigned owners and worker/scheduler supervision are verified. Keep email activation off until the real sender and policies are agreed. No new scheduler or untracked process is required: use the existing supervised `tasker_scheduler` and `tasker_worker` commands and the jobs-health screen. Recurrence and report SMTP jobs share the current leasing, reference payload and retry infrastructure.

For rollback, first disable live report email and recurrence and restart affected workers/scheduler so no previously loaded process keeps operating. Drain/review pending work, then disable the improvement switch and restore matching application code/templates if needed. Keep additive tables, SOP versions, occurrences, declarations and delivery uncertainty intact. Existing generated tasks remain ordinary tasks. Do not erase dedupe receipts, reset UNKNOWN deliveries or reverse evidence migrations as a routine rollback. Disabling improvement restores legacy private-review/report submission behavior; review that transition with the operating owner.
